Quick Answer

An OEM or private-label inquiry needs more than a logo. Buyers should define the attachment and target excavator, supply fitment and hydraulic data, explain the application, provide quantity and branding files, and identify document, packing and destination requirements. Availability must be confirmed for the selected product, quantity and production method. Production should not be approved until final technical and commercial details have been verified.

What OEM and Private Label Mean for Excavator Attachments

In attachment purchasing, OEM can describe a supplier producing an attachment or configuration against an agreed buyer requirement. Private label usually focuses on buyer-facing brand elements such as a logo, color reference, model name or nameplate. ODM is often used for a supplier-based product that receives agreed changes, but terminology varies between companies.

These labels do not prove that a requested change is feasible. The exact product basis, design responsibility, approved drawings, branding scope, testing evidence, documentation and commercial terms must be defined project by project. A 15-Step OEM Buyer Workflow

  1. Select the attachment category.
  2. Provide the excavator brand and model.
  3. Provide the carrier operating weight.
  4. Confirm pin diameter, center distance and dipper width where required.
  5. Confirm hydraulic flow and pressure for hydraulic attachments.
  6. Provide the expected application.
  7. Provide the quantity.
  8. Provide the branding files.
  9. Confirm the color reference.
  10. Confirm the nameplate requirements.
  11. Confirm the manual and document language.
  12. Confirm the packaging requirements.
  13. Confirm the destination country and port.
  14. Review the technical confirmation documents.
  15. Approve production only after final verification.

Step 1: Define the Attachment and Target Excavator

Name the attachment category, intended material, task, duty pattern, carrier model and operating weight. If several carrier models are involved, list each separately. Do not assume one bracket or hydraulic setup fits every excavator in a weight class.

Step 2: Confirm Technical Matching Information

For mechanical attachments, prepare the excavator model, operating weight, pin diameter, pin-center distance, dipper or arm width, coupler type, required working width or capacity, and application. For hydraulic attachments, also provide auxiliary hydraulic flow, operating pressure, return-line requirements, hose and fitting standards, electrical requirements where applicable, and a rotation circuit where applicable.

The hydraulic flow and pressure guide explains why carrier data must be checked against the exact attachment configuration. There are no universal dimensions or hydraulic settings for OEM projects.

Step 3: Confirm Branding Requirements

Prepare a vector logo file, exact brand-name spelling, proposed logo position, preferred color reference, nameplate information, product model naming, packaging marks, manual language, and barcode or label requirements where applicable. Availability must be confirmed for the selected product, quantity and production method.

Step 4: Confirm Color and Appearance Requirements

Use a traceable color reference and show which surfaces should carry that color. Confirm whether visible fasteners, hoses, wear surfaces and safety markings are excluded. Exact paint systems, preparation requirements, gloss, layer thickness and acceptance standards are [BUSINESS INPUT REQUIRED].

Step 5: Confirm Nameplate and Documentation Requirements

State the legal brand name, model naming, serial-number format request, technical fields, language and regulatory information the buyer expects. The supplier must confirm what can appear on the nameplate and in manuals. Do not place unverified certification marks or unsupported technical claims in artwork.

Step 6: Confirm Packaging and Shipping Requirements

Provide destination country and port, handling constraints, packaging marks, label or barcode requirements, mixed-model request, document language and any consignee instructions. Available packaging methods, container planning, consolidation, delivery time and shipping documents are [BUSINESS INPUT REQUIRED] until confirmed for the order.

Step 7: Technical Review and Drawing Confirmation

Review the attachment model, carrier interface, dimensions, hydraulic connections, accessories, rotation or electrical requirements and application assumptions in one controlled confirmation set. Mark revisions clearly. Do not approve production from an informal image or an earlier drawing when the final configuration has changed.

Step 8: Sample or Pre-Production Approval Where Applicable

A sample, first article, rendering, label proof, color sample, nameplate proof or pre-production photograph may be appropriate depending on project risk. Whether KRATOR offers any specific approval method, and the cost, time or acceptance criteria, are [BUSINESS INPUT REQUIRED].

Step 9: Production and Quality-Control Checkpoints

Generally applicable checkpoints may include incoming-material identification, dimensional inspection, welding inspection where applicable, machining inspection, hydraulic connection inspection, assembly inspection, surface preparation, paint and appearance inspection, nameplate and branding verification, functional testing where applicable, and packing verification.

These are a buyer review framework, not a claim that every product follows every checkpoint or test. Step 10: Packing, Loading and Shipment Documents

Before release, reconcile model, quantity, accessories, nameplates, branding, labels, packaging marks and the approved document list. Buyers should request evidence appropriate to the order, but the available photos, packing records, loading evidence and shipment documents must be confirmed commercially.

Common OEM Purchasing Mistakes

  • Starting with logo artwork before choosing a technically suitable attachment.
  • Using excavator tonnage alone and omitting pin dimensions or hydraulic data.
  • Sending a raster screenshot when production artwork requires a controlled vector file.
  • Leaving color, nameplate, manual or packaging requirements until after production approval.
  • Assuming private labeling is available for every product and quantity.
  • Approving an outdated drawing or incomplete configuration.
  • Failing to define who reviews spelling, model names, labels and required documents.
  • Assuming delivery time, MOQ, warranty or exclusivity without written confirmation.

What Distributors Should Verify Before Placing an Order

Verify the final product configuration, carrier fit, hydraulic interfaces, accessories and spares; branding files and proofs; nameplate and manual content; inspection and approval evidence; packing and label requirements; quantity and destination documents; and all commercial terms. Keep approved revisions in one project record and identify who has authority to release production.

Dealer Technical-Support Requirements

Excavator attachment dealers, importers, rental fleets, equipment brands, regional distributors, and repair or service companies may need different support. Typical requests can include multiple attachment categories, mixed model orders, model-based technical data, spare-parts planning, sales materials, manuals, consolidated shipping and recurring orders. Each item remains subject to availability and project review; this guide does not promise exclusivity, territory protection, stock programs or distributor discounts.

Intellectual Property and Brand-File Preparation

Provide only brand files the buyer is authorized to use. Name files clearly, record the approved version, and define the review contact for spelling, logo placement, color, nameplate, manual and packaging marks. Ownership, licensing, confidentiality, tooling, drawing reuse and other intellectual-property terms are [BUSINESS INPUT REQUIRED] and should be handled in project documents rather than inferred from this guide.

VISIBLE FAQ

OEM Excavator Attachment Questions

Answers depend on the exact product, carrier, quantity, branding request and commercial review.

What information is needed for an OEM excavator attachment quotation?

Provide the attachment type, excavator model and operating weight, fitment dimensions where required, hydraulic data for hydraulic attachments, application, quantity, branding files, destination and document requirements. Final requirements depend on the selected product and project review.

Can an excavator attachment carry a distributor's logo?

A distributor logo may be considered for some products and order methods, but availability, placement, production method and approval must be confirmed for the selected product and quantity before order confirmation.

Can product colors and nameplates be customized?

Color references and nameplate content may be submitted for review. Feasibility, finish, artwork format and any product-specific restrictions require technical and commercial confirmation.

What excavator dimensions are required before production?

Where fitment requires them, buyers should provide pin diameter, pin-center distance and dipper or arm width, plus coupler details. The exact dimensions required vary by attachment and mounting method.

Is a sample required before a bulk order?

A sample or pre-production approval may be appropriate for some projects, but it is not universal. The need, scope, cost and approval method remain [BUSINESS INPUT REQUIRED] and must be agreed before production.

Can different attachment models be shipped together?

Mixed-model or consolidated shipping may be requested, but feasibility depends on product combinations, quantity, packing, documentation and shipping arrangements. It must be confirmed for the specific order.

What files should be provided for private-label branding?

Prepare a vector logo, confirmed brand spelling, color reference, desired logo position, nameplate content, model naming, packaging marks, manual language and barcode or label requirements where applicable.

How is OEM compatibility confirmed before production?

Compatibility should be reviewed from the excavator, mounting dimensions, hydraulic requirements, application and selected attachment configuration. Production should be approved only after the buyer and supplier verify the final technical confirmation documents.
