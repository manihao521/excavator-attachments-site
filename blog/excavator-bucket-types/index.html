Buckets

Excavator Bucket Types: Standard, Rock, Skeleton and Tilt Buckets

Bucket type should match soil, rock, material size and finishing requirements. Wear package and width matter as much as bucket volume.

7 min readKRATOR Technical TeamUpdated 2026
On This Page
Quick AnswerBucket Selection FactorsMatching TableCommon MistakesWhat to Send for QuoteRelated ProductsFAQ
Quick Answer

Use standard buckets for general soil, rock buckets for abrasive digging, skeleton buckets for sorting and screening, and tilt buckets for grading or ditch cleaning.

Bucket Selection Factors

Material condition

Soil, gravel, rock and debris need different structures.

Wear package

Teeth, side cutters and wear strips extend service life.

Bucket width

Width affects digging force, capacity and finishing quality.

Screening need

Skeleton buckets sort rocks and debris from soil.

Grading need

Tilt buckets help shape slopes and clean ditches.

Pin dimensions

Bracket and pin size must match excavator arm.

Why Material condition Matters

Soil, gravel, rock and debris need different structures. This point should be confirmed before production so the attachment can be matched to the real carrier and jobsite.

How to Check Wear package

Teeth, side cutters and wear strips extend service life. Clear photos, machine information and simple notes make the matching process faster for overseas buyers.

Final Check Before Ordering

Confirm excavator model, operating weight, attachment configuration, hydraulic requirement, dimensions if available, quantity and destination before placing the order.

Common Mistakes

  • Choosing by price or product name without checking excavator data.
  • Not sending photos, dimensions or hydraulic information when they are needed.
  • Ignoring jobsite material, working condition and duty cycle.
  • Forgetting spare parts, bracket matching and shipping details.

What Information to Send for Quote

  • Excavator brand and model
  • Operating weight
  • Attachment type
  • Working condition
  • Photos or drawings
  • Quantity and destination
