Hydraulic Breaker Spare Parts Buying Guide

Identify hydraulic breaker wear, sealing, hydraulic and structural parts using the exact breaker model, nameplate evidence and part details before requesting a match.

Quick Answer

To order hydraulic breaker spare parts, first confirm the breaker brand, exact model and serial or nameplate information. Then identify the suspected part, provide clear installed and removed-part photos, record safely accessible dimensions only when requested, explain the replacement reason, and confirm quantity and destination. Appearance alone is not enough: chisels, seal kits, bushings, pistons and bolts that look similar may still use different dimensions or designs.

Why Breaker Model Identification Comes First

A spare-parts inquiry should begin with the breaker, not with a generic component name. Model names can vary by market, and a hydraulic breaker product family may contain several sizes or revisions. The nameplate, serial information where available and clear views of the complete breaker help establish which documentation and part family should be checked.

Do not assume that parts from different brands, models or housing configurations are interchangeable. A similar external shape does not confirm the shank, bore, retaining system, seal arrangement, thread, port or internal fit. If the breaker identity is uncertain, send evidence first and wait for model confirmation before ordering.

Hydraulic Breaker Spare-Parts Overview

Common inquiry categories include working tools, bushings, retaining parts, seals, fasteners, pistons, head and cylinder components, accumulator-related parts where fitted, hoses, fittings and mounting hardware. Component design and naming vary by breaker brand and model. Not every breaker contains every listed component, and a category name alone is not a compatibility decision.

Wear Parts Versus Structural and Hydraulic Parts

Wear parts are components expected to be inspected regularly because they contact the working tool, material or moving interfaces. Working tools, some bushings and retaining components are common examples, but replacement criteria still come from the correct breaker documentation. A visible mark does not by itself establish a wear limit.

Sealing, hydraulic and structural parts require a different level of evidence. A seal kit, piston, cylinder, front head, back head or accumulator-related component should not be selected from a general photograph. Internal dismantling, seal replacement, accumulator work and nitrogen-chamber work are outside this buying guide and must be performed by qualified technicians using the correct service information.

Common Hydraulic Breaker Spare-Part Groups

Working Tool / Chisel

The working tool must match the breaker model, shank and retainer design as well as the intended material. Diameter and overall length are useful only as part of a complete model-specific check. For working-end selection and replacement evidence, use the hydraulic breaker chisel guide.

Front and Lower Bushings

Bushing position and naming vary. If abnormal tool movement or visible wear is suspected, isolate the equipment and record the condition without improvising a clearance limit. A qualified technician should inspect and measure the parts using the correct manual.

Retaining Pins and Stoppers

Retaining pins, stop pins and related parts should be identified by their installed location, profile and model context. Send photos of both the component and its mating area when safely available. Damaged retaining or structural parts should not be reused.

Seal Kits

A hydraulic breaker seal kit is model-specific and may also depend on a version or serial range. Provide the nameplate, existing kit marking or verified part number where available, and the service diagnosis. Do not open the breaker to identify seals without an approved procedure.

Through Bolts and Side Bolts

Through-bolt and side-bolt terminology is not universal. Confirm the fastener location, thread and dimensions requested for the exact model. Do not substitute a visually similar bolt or publish torque values without the approved model documentation.

Piston

The breaker piston is a precision internal part. Matching requires the exact breaker identity and qualified inspection evidence. Do not grind, weld, rework or reuse a damaged piston based on an unverified assessment.

Front Head, Cylinder and Back Head

These major components may contain model-specific bores, passages and interfaces. A supplier may require serial information, casting or part markings, photos and additional approved measurements. Structural damage requires technician review and should not be treated as a routine wear-part purchase.

Accumulator and Diaphragm Components Where Applicable

Some breaker designs include accumulator-related components; others differ. Do not open an accumulator or nitrogen chamber, release pressure, or order a diaphragm from appearance alone. Identification and service must follow the exact model documentation.

Hoses, Fittings and Connection Parts

Record the breaker and carrier model, hose route, fitting location and visible condition. Hydraulic specification and connection details must be verified. The hydraulic flow and pressure guide explains why carrier data should be confirmed rather than inferred.

Mounting Bracket, Pins and Bushings

Mounting parts relate to both the breaker bracket and the excavator interface. Prepare the excavator brand, model, operating weight and safely measured pin data. Use the breaker selection guide when the underlying carrier-breaker match also needs review.

Common Spare-Parts Ordering Mistakes

  • Ordering from a photo without the breaker brand, exact model or nameplate.
  • Assuming a part is interchangeable because it looks similar.
  • Using only a generic part name such as seal kit, piston or bolt.
  • Sending one measurement without the part profile and installed location.
  • Mixing parts for several breaker models in one unlabelled photo or list.
  • Requesting an internal component without qualified inspection evidence.
  • Reusing a visibly cracked, deformed or damaged structural part.
  • Omitting quantity, destination country or port from a commercial inquiry.

If a part request follows a no-strike, weak-impact, overheating or leakage symptom, use the safe breaker troubleshooting sequence first. Keep fault diagnosis separate from the purchasing decision.

How to Identify the Correct Replacement Part

  1. Confirm breaker brand and exact model. Do not rely on a housing color or general size.
  2. Photograph the nameplate. Include serial information where available and readable.
  3. Record excavator model and carrier weight. Add hydraulic configuration only when relevant to the part or diagnosis.
  4. Identify the suspected part. State its name and installed location without guessing an internal failure.
  5. Photograph the installed component. Include context plus close views from multiple angles.
  6. Record dimensions only where safely accessible. Follow the requested measurement method and never dismantle a pressurized assembly for a buying inquiry.
  7. Provide old part markings or part number if available. Markings support identification but still require model confirmation.
  8. Describe the fault or replacement reason. State the visible condition and when it began.
  9. Confirm quantity. Separate quantities by model and part.
  10. Provide destination country and port. Add the preferred transport method only if known.

Appearance alone is not enough for compatibility. Similar-looking parts may use different dimensions, model names can vary by market, and serial or nameplate data may be necessary. Parts should not be ordered solely from an unverified photo.

Hydraulic Breaker Spare-Parts FAQ

These answers keep identification model-specific and separate purchasing evidence from qualified internal service work.

What information is needed to order hydraulic breaker spare parts?

Start with the breaker brand, exact model, serial number if available and a clear nameplate photo. Add the part name, existing marking or part number if available, quantity, safely accessible dimensions, multiple photos, replacement reason and destination. A supplier may request more model-specific evidence before confirming a match.

Are hydraulic breaker parts interchangeable between brands?

Do not assume interchangeability between brands, model families or market versions. Similar-looking parts can use different dimensions, seal arrangements, retainers, ports or internal designs. Compatibility should be confirmed from the exact breaker identity and verified part information.

How can I identify the correct hydraulic breaker seal kit?

Identify the exact breaker brand and model first, then provide the serial or nameplate data and any verified kit marking or part number. Do not select a seal kit from appearance alone, and do not open the breaker or replace internal seals without the correct service documentation and a qualified technician.

When should a breaker bushing be replaced?

Replacement criteria and permitted clearance are model-specific. Record visible wear or unusual tool movement after safe shutdown, then have a qualified technician measure and inspect the bushing using the correct breaker documentation. Do not reuse a cracked, deformed or otherwise damaged structural component.

Can I order a chisel using only its diameter and length?

Diameter and length alone are not enough. The breaker model, shank profile, retaining arrangement, slot position, working-end shape and other model-specific dimensions may also matter. Send a nameplate photo and clear tool photos with the requested measurements.

What is the difference between a through bolt and a side bolt?

The names generally refer to different fasteners used in the breaker assembly or housing, but terminology and design vary by brand and model. Identify the fastener location, breaker model, visible marking and dimensions requested by the supplier rather than ordering by name alone.

Should a damaged breaker piston be repaired or replaced?

A piston is an internal precision component. Do not make a repair decision from an external photo or attempt improvised grinding, welding or reuse. A qualified technician should inspect it against the correct breaker documentation and determine the approved action.

Can KRATOR help prepare a mixed spare-parts order?

KRATOR can review a mixed inquiry when each breaker and part is identified clearly. Send a model-by-model list, quantities, nameplate photos, part photos and destination details. Available categories, supported models, packing, minimum quantities and shipping options require business confirmation before quotation.
